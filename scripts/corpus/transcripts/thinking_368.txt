Writing goals is easy. Achieving them is the hard part. You’ll see this later in the episode where I talk about my 2025 successes and failures.
Let’s start with a simple framework that I always recommend: SMART Goals . This means choosing goals that are Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant , and Time Bound .
Instead of vague resolutions like, “I want to get better at English”, try something more concrete , something more specific. “ I will complete three listening exercises each week ” or “ I will learn 10 new words every Friday. “
When your goal is clear and achievable, you are far more likely to follow through and keep your goal.
I’d also recommend that you try setting both personal and professional or academic goals. Maybe you want to “ improve your health ” or “ learn a new skill ” and at the same time “ push forward ” in your studies or career.
Having a balance between the two gives you a more complete sense of progress.
One of the biggest challenges is sticking to your goals over time. And I think the secret here is tracking . You need a system that regularly reminds you what you are working toward . Tracking your goals helps. You write your goals down in a notebook or maybe a calendar, and then you regularly review and adjust your progress toward that goal.
Ask yourself… “ What worked this month?” “What didn’t work?” “What small change could I make next month to make it easier?”
Tracking keeps you honest and it keeps you motivated.
Another important tip is to break your goals into smaller actions. Big goals, massive targets, often fail because we feel overwhelmed . But small steps can help you build momentum.
Also create an environment that helps you succeed. Remove any obstacles or friction . Make your habits obvious. For example, if you want to study English in the morning, put your notebook or your listening headphones on your desk the night before. If you want to exercise more, pack your gym bag ahead of time or lay out your clothes.
Make the goal as easy as possible to start.
For example, last year I wanted to eat out less. I wanted to eat my lunch at home every day. So one of the easiest ways to do this is to make my lunch the night before. So when I’m making dinner, I also make lunch. And then, I don’t have to worry. It’s so easy just to eat my lunchbox every day.
The most important tip I want to give you, though, is to be flexible .
Life will change. Your goals will change. You will have weeks where nothing goes to plan . But it is also okay if your goals change or adjust slightly. You’ll see in a few seconds that I had some unexpected success this year in certain areas, which refocused my plans and my priorities. And all of this is okay, be flexible.
