Singapore is one of the wealthiest and most modern countries in the world, but that wasn’t always the case.
Today, I want to discuss the history of Singapore. We’ll look at its early role in Asian trade, British colonial rule, the Japanese occupation, independence, and its transformation into one of the world’s richest and most developed countries.
We’ll also explore the political and social model behind Singapore’s success and ask whether its unique approach can continue into the future.
At the same time, we will learn some new vocabulary and practice our English listening comprehension at the same time.
Singapore is a small island located at the southern tip of the Malay Peninsula in Southeast Asia. It is right next to the southern part of modern-day Malaysia. To the south, across the Singapore Strait , are the islands of Indonesia.
Historically, this has been a very important location. Singapore sits really close to the Strait of Malacca. The Strait of Malacca is a long, narrow waterway which separates the Malay Peninsula from the Indonesian island of Sumatra.
The strait connects the Indian Ocean with the South China Sea. This means the Strait of Malacca connects parts of South Asia, Southeast Asia, the Middle East, and East Asia.
Without the Strait of Malacca, a ship traveling from India towards China would need to sail around the entire Indonesian archipelago . The Strait of Malacca, though, provides a relatively direct route between the Indian Ocean and the South China Sea.
For centuries, ships carrying valuable goods, like spices and silk, porcelain, textiles, and metal, passed through this region. The ships needed a place to stop on their route. They needed somewhere to get fresh water and food. They needed a place to repair their ships and wait during storms.
As a result, settlements along the Strait of Malacca started to become wealthy and politically significant. Singapore was one of these settlements.
Singapore has been known by several different names in the past. One of the most important names was Temasek. Historians do not have a complete record of what life was like on the island when it was known as Temasek, but archeological evidence shows that the island was part of wider trading networks in Southeast Asia.
At different points in history, the region was influenced by powerful kingdoms and empires, including the Srivijaya and later the Majapahit. Srivijaya was a powerful kingdom based in what is now Indonesia. From roughly the 7th to the 13th centuries, it controlled or influenced important parts of the Strait of Malacca. And later, the Majapahit Empire, which was based in Java, modern-day Indonesia as well, became a powerful force too.
These kingdoms, though, were not modern nation-states . They didn’t have borders like we think of right now. Political power in medieval Asia worked really differently. The important point, though, is that Singapore was part of a connected world. People were already traveling through the region, through the area, and were trading with each other.
By the beginning of the 19th century, European powers were becoming interested in Southeast Asia, too. Britain and the Netherlands, in particular, were competing for influence over the region’s trade.
Britain was, of course, a global trading power, a massive empire with colonies and commercial interests in India and China. British merchants wanted a reliable port where their ship could stop and trade around Asia. And if you remember, the Strait of Malacca, close to where Singapore is, is the best way to travel by ship from India to China.
The Dutch also had a very strong position in Southeast Asia. The Dutch were present in Indonesia, in the Indonesian archipelago, and controlled other trading centers in the region, too.
The British already had a settlement at Penang in the northern part of the Malay Peninsula, but they wanted another port further south.
In 1819, a British official named Stamford Raffles arrived in Singapore. Raffles was working for the British East India Company. The East India Company was a powerful organization.
I think most people think of colonialism as something governments do. The British government colonized the world . But actually, private companies like the East India Company were often the people doing the colonizing.
Raffles recognized that Singapore could be a useful trading post. He negotiated an agreement with local Malay rulers that allowed the British East India Company to establish a settlement on the island.
The British declared Singapore a free port . A free port is a port where traders can buy, sell, import and export without any taxes or restrictions, or with just a few taxes and restrictions.
If you have two possible ports where you can sell your goods, and one charges high taxes while the other allows you to trade freely , the second port, the one that lets you trade for free, becomes much more attractive . So Singapore became attractive . Ships started to arrive from places such as India and China, even the Middle East.
When the ships arrived in Singapore, they needed warehouses . They needed banks and insurance companies. They needed workers to load and unload their cargo . They needed shops and markets and restaurants and transport and different services for the people working on the ships. All of these industries developed around the port of Singapore.
People also came to Singapore looking for work . Chinese immigrants eventually became the largest ethnic group. Indian and Malay communities also became important parts of Singaporean society, and there were also Europeans and people from other parts of Asia, too.
In 1826, Singapore was grouped together with Britain’s other ports in the region, Penang and Malacca, to form the Straits Settlement, which were administered by the British. These three settlements were really strategically important. They were all located along the Strait of Malacca.
In 1867, the Straits Settlements became a British colony, meaning that they were placed directly under the control of the British government rather than being controlled by the East India Company.
There is also a famous legend about how Singapore got its name. According to the traditional story, a prince traveled to the island. When he arrived, he saw an unusual animal. His advisors identified the animal as a lion, and the prince decided to establish a settlement there.
He gave the settlement the name Singapura , which comes from Sanskrit, “singa” meaning lion and “pura” meaning city or town. Lion City.
There is a big problem with this story, though. Lions have never been native to Singapore. It could have been a tiger, which are native to the region.
One reason Singapore became so successful was that it was a place where goods were moved, exchanged, stored, financed, and transported it could do everything.
Two particularly important products were tin and rubber. Tin was used in industry, and rubber became important due to things like tires and more industrialization. Much of Southeast Asia’s tin and rubber came from the Malay Peninsula, so Singapore was the perfect place to export these commodities .
Singapore became a middleman for global trade. It didn’t produce the resources, and Singapore didn’t really buy resources either, but it was a place for the producers and the buyers to do their business.
As I just mentioned, Singapore also had a very multicultural society , but the society wasn’t socially equal really. British authorities categorized the population according to ethnicity. Different communities lived in different neighborhoods. There were areas associated with Chinese communities, Indian communities, and Malay communities. Europeans had their own part of the city and generally enjoyed a higher social position.
The growth of trade changed Singapore physically. They built roads and docks and schools and hospitals and other infrastructure. As the port expanded, new commercial districts were built around the port.
Banks provided loans and handled payments. Insurance companies protected merchants against the risk of shipping. Warehouses stored goods before they were transported elsewhere.
At the beginning of the Second World War, Singapore was still one of Britain’s most important bases in Asia.
But in February 1942, the island was occupied by Japan. Japan had invaded British-controlled Malaya from the north and advanced down the peninsula.
On the 15th of February 1942, British forces surrendered Singapore to Japan. Japan occupied Singapore for more than three years. That occupation was extremely harsh. One of the most notorious events was the Sook Ching Massacre, during which the Japanese military carried out mass killings of people suspected of being anti-Japanese, especially among Singapore’s Chinese population.
The occupation ended in 1945 when Japan surrendered at the end of the Second World War. The occupation demonstrated that the British were not invincible. This weakened the control of the British and strengthened the demand for greater political control by Singapore’s own people.
After the Second World War ended, the British did return to Singapore, but the situation was different from before the war.
Across Asia, anti-colonial movements were growing. People wanted the right to be independent.
Different groups in Singapore had different ideas about the island’s future. Some people wanted gradual reform. Others demanded independence right now. Trade unions and left-wing organizations became influential, but so did anti-communism figures .
Definitely the most important figure in post-war Singapore was an anti-communist figure, Lee Kuan Yew .
In 1954, Lee and some other people founded the People’s Action Party, or PAP. This is the political party that dominates Singaporean politics.
In 1959, Singapore achieved internal self-government. This meant that Singaporeans could elect a government to manage most of their own affairs , but Britain still retained some control over defense and foreign policy.
The PAP won the 1959 election, and Lee Kuan Yew became Singapore’s first prime minister. This was a big step towards independence, but Singapore was not an independent country yet.
Lee Kuan Yew believed that Singapore might struggle to survive as an independent country. The island was small and had limited resources.
At the time, the Federation of Malaya was already an independent country. There was an idea to create a larger federation that could include Malaya, Singapore, and also the other British territories of Sabah and Sarawak on the island of Borneo.
After several years of negotiations, Singapore joined the new Federation of Malaysia on the 16th of September 1963.
But the relationship was difficult immediately. There were major disagreements between Singapore’s government and Malaysia’s federal government.
Malaysia’s political system gave special political privileges and recognition to ethnically Malay people. Singapore was different from the rest of Malaysia, though, in that it had a majority non-Malay population. Singapore is majority Chinese.
In 1964, racial riots broke out in Singapore between Malay and Chinese communities, resulting in deaths and injuries.
Then in August 1965, Malaysia’s parliament voted to remove Singapore from the federation. Singapore became an independent country on August 9th, 1965. This is one of the unusual features of Singaporean history. Independence was not really the result of a Singaporean independence movement.
Under Lee Kuan Yew, the government took an active role in shaping Singapore’s development. The government invested in infrastructure, education, housing, healthcare, and public administration. This approach is sometimes described as state-led development . The basic idea is that the government actively tries to create the conditions for economic growth.
In the 1960s, many Singaporeans lived in overcrowded housing. The government constructed a large number of public houses , affordable public houses . Today, the vast majority of Singaporeans live in planned public housing.
Housing policy also had a political purpose. The government wanted different ethnic groups to live alongside each other rather than being separated into different communities.
The government also invested in education. Singapore built more schools and universities. There was a big focus on subjects like mathematics and science. English became particularly important, actually.
English allowed Singapore to communicate with international businesses and also be a neutral working language. In a society that was divided between Chinese, Malay, and Indian communities, English was a neutral language in between them.
The government created a strong military and introduced national service . Male citizens were required, still are required, to join the Singaporean military.
At the same time, the government placed enormous emphasis on stability and bureaucracy. International companies would be more willing to invest in Singapore if they believed that Singapore was safe, predictable, and well-governed. This became the central part of Singapore’s strategy.
The policies that Singapore introduced after becoming independent produced remarkable results. Singapore went from a relatively poor country to one of the world’s richest and wealthiest economies. The transformation is sometimes described as the Singapore economic miracle .
Singapore moved away from simply being a trading port. They did not have large resources of oil or coal or iron or anything natural. It could not become wealthy by exporting raw materials in the way some other countries had done in the past. It had to make itself an attractive place for international companies to operate.
Gradually, Singapore’s economy moved towards higher value economic activities. The Singaporean economy today is focused on sectors like banking and finance, international trade, shipping and logistics, technology and pharmaceuticals, and professional services.
Singapore is also in a very convenient position. Its port became one of the world’s most important. Changi Airport helped establish Singapore as a major international aviation hub . It’s a place where if you want to travel around the world, Singapore was a place you could stop.
Singapore’s economic transformation is closely connected to Lee Kuan Yew. Lee served as the Prime Minister of Singapore from 1959 until 1990, and remained an influential figure for many years afterwards. Under his leadership, his political party, the People’s Action Party, became the dominant political force in Singapore. The PAP has won every general election since Singapore achieved self-government.
Singapore is an example of a country whose political system is very different from many Western democracies, but they still manage to develop.
Singapore has elections and a parliament and political parties and a constitution. Citizens can vote, and opposition parties have won seats in parliament. But the PAP maintains an overwhelming amount of political power and dominance for decades. There is a difference between having elections and having a genuinely competitive political system.
The Singaporean government argues that this political stability has been important for the country’s success. A stable government can pursue long-term policies without having to worry about losing power. Businesses can have confidence that policies won’t change quickly.
But what about political freedom?
The Singaporean government has placed restrictions on political activity and the freedom of expression in the past. There have also been concerns about the treatment of opposition politicians and the independence of Singapore’s media. The government has generally defended these policies by arguing that Singapore needs its stability.
Singapore’s political system is often described as somewhere in the middle of a liberal Western democracy and an authoritarian state. It has elections and political institutions, but it also has a dominant ruling party and significant restrictions on political activity.
Singapore’s transformation was not only economic. Singapore officially recognizes four broad ethnic categories: Chinese, Malay, Indian, and other. The government has used these categories as part of its approach to managing diversity. The aim has always been to prevent Singapore from becoming a society in which different ethnic communities live separate lives.
Singapore is now a major center for finance, shipping, aviation, technology, international business. Its port is one of the world’s most important. Changi Airport connects the city to destinations across the globe. And the Mass Rapid Transport system provides efficient public transport.
The result is that Singapore often appears as a clean, organized, and efficient city.
Much of Singapore’s environment was deliberately planned by the state. This is another important feature of the Singaporean model. The state has attempted to manage the economy and the physical and social development of the country.
Singapore is multicultural, but the state actively manages ethnic relations. Singapore has a modern economy, but the government plays a role in planning that economy.
Singapore’s history is remarkable. Singapore started as a trading settlement in Southeast Asia and is now one of the world’s wealthiest and most modern cities. Along the way, it was a British colony, part of Malaysia, and an independent country that seemed unlikely to succeed.
But Singapore’s future will bring more challenges. It’s going to have an aging population, it’s going to have to deal with rising living costs, and questions about its political system. The country will need to find a way to keep its prosperity and stability around these changes.
